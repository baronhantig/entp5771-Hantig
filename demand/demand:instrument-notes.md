# **Ohsnap Wallet 2 Demand Instrument Notes**

## **Part 1 \- Elicitation Choice**

I chose yes/no demand because the relevant customer decision is whether to purchase one Bare Bones Ohsnap Wallet 2 during a 12-month decision period. A buyer might eventually replace a wallet, but the same buyer would not ordinarily purchase several identical Bare Bones wallets for personal use within one year. The instrument therefore asks for the maximum the respondent would pay for one unit during the next 12 months rather than asking how many units the respondent would purchase. This matches the previously submitted decision question about whether at least 30% of qualified potential customers unfamiliar with Ohsnap would be willing to purchase one Bare Bones Wallet 2 at its $60 MSRP.

The survey does not ask directly, "Would you buy this for $60?" Instead, it collects each qualified respondent's maximum willingness to pay without first revealing the $60 benchmark. During analysis, a response of $60 or more will count as stated willingness to purchase at the MSRP. The share of qualified respondents at or above $60 can then be compared with the 30% decision threshold. If at least 30% meet or exceed $60, the result supports considering production and sale beyond Kickstarter. If fewer than 30% do, the same commitment is not supported, and the price, configuration, target customer, or production scale should be reconsidered.

## **Eligibility Definition**

For this instrument, a **qualified potential customer unfamiliar with Ohsnap** is someone who:

1. Is 18 or older.  
2. Had not heard of Ohsnap or the Ohsnap Wallet 2 before receiving the survey.  
3. Personally choose the wallet or card holder they use.  
4. Carries physical cards on at least three days in a typical week.  
5. Could plausibly purchase or replace a wallet or card holder during the next 12 months.

These criteria screen for the customer's purchase decision rather than the founder's production decision. Respondents who do not meet every condition are routed to the end before seeing the product, image, or valuation questions.

## **Planned Segmentation**

The characteristics section is limited to variables that will be used in analysis:

* **Current wallet type:** Compare willingness to pay among traditional-wallet, minimalist-wallet, metal/pop-up-wallet, and phone-wallet users.  
* **Number of cards carried:** Compare reactions across the 3-, 5-, and 7-card capacity ranges and identify whether the offering fits respondents' actual carrying needs.  
* **Amount paid for current wallet:** Compare maximum willingness to pay with demonstrated past spending on the same product category.  
* **Age range:** Check whether appeal and willingness to pay differ meaningfully across adult age groups.

Names, email addresses, gender, income, exact location, and other personal information are omitted because they are not necessary for the planned analysis.

## **Part 3 \- Read as a Respondent**

### **1\. I might not know whether I count as a "qualified customer."**

**Problem found:** The phrase "qualified potential customer" is meaningful to the project team but vague to a respondent. Different people could interpret it differently.

**Change made:** I replaced that phrase with four concrete screening questions about prior familiarity, personal responsibility for choosing a wallet, physical-card use, and possible purchase within the next 12 months. Each question has explicit branching.

### **2\. I might be under 18 but continue because the survey looks harmless.**

**Problem found:** Without an age gate, a minor could answer questions about a purchase decision that may actually belong to a parent or guardian.

**Change made:** Age is the first required question. A "No" response routes immediately to the end before the respondent sees any other question.

### **3\. Prior knowledge of Ohsnap could influence my reaction.**

**Problem found:** Existing customers or people familiar with the Kickstarter campaign may already know the price, brand, features, or campaign success. Their answers would not test the submitted question about customers unfamiliar with Ohsnap.

**Change made:** I added a prior-familiarity screen before showing the product. "Yes" and "Not sure" both route to the end so the final sample remains conservative and clearly unfamiliar.

### **4\. The Kickstarter page would tell me what price answer is expected.**

**Problem found:** The campaign page displays a $45 Kickstarter price, a $60 MSRP, a 25% discount, and backer totals. Showing any of those before valuation would anchor the respondent and could make campaign popularity substitute for personal judgment.

**Change made:** I removed all prices, discounts, campaign totals, testimonials, and popularity signals from the offering section. The $60 benchmark appears only in these analysis notes, not in the respondent-facing survey.

### **5\. The campaign's marketing language might push me toward a positive answer.**

**Problem found:** Phrases such as "impossibly thin," "everything you need," and "exclusive discount" are sales claims rather than neutral specifications.

**Change made:** I rewrote the description using factual details: two aluminum covers, one interchangeable card core, 3/5/7-card choices, color choice, and a lever that fans the cards. I also state what the Bare Bones configuration does not include.

### **6\. The image itself could reveal the campaign price or promote a different configuration.**

**Problem found:** A screenshot of the reward page contains the $45 pledge, $60 MSRP, discount language, and nearby images of MagSafe configurations. It could both anchor price and confuse which product is being valued.

**Change made:** The form instructions require one cropped, product-only image of the Bare Bones configuration with all price, reward, slogan, backer, and accessory information removed.

### **7\. I might not know exactly which version I am pricing.**

**Problem found:** The campaign contains several reward tiers with MagSafe, added storage, and premium cover materials. Calling the product only "Wallet 2" could cause respondents to value different bundles.

**Change made:** The description repeatedly names the **Bare Bones** configuration, identifies the selectable 3/5/7-card core and color, and explicitly excludes MagSafe, additional cash/storage components, and premium cover materials.

### **8\. "Would you buy it?" might feel like a request to support the project.**

**Problem found:** A yes/no purchase question could encourage a polite answer and would not show how demand changes with price.

**Change made:** The form asks for a numerical maximum willingness to pay. The context states that there are no right or wrong answers and does not claim that the product will be produced.

### **9\. I might think I am negotiating and deliberately give a lower price.**

**Problem found:** Asking for price immediately after the description could make the interaction feel like bargaining.

**Change made:** The required appeal rating and three open-ended feedback questions appear before valuation, framing the interaction as product feedback before money is discussed.

### **10\. I might think zero is invalid or unhelpful.**

**Problem found:** A respondent who would not buy the wallet at any price might guess a small positive number if the form appears to require one.

**Change made:** The valuation help text explicitly says to enter **0** if the respondent would not purchase at any price. The field uses numerical validation with a minimum of zero and no slider or positive-number requirement. The preview checklist requires a test submission and CSV export containing zero.

### **11\. The time period could be interpreted differently by different respondents.**

**Problem found:** "What is the most you would pay for a wallet?" does not distinguish an immediate purchase from a replacement that might happen years later.

**Change made:** Both the screener and valuation question specify the same decision period: **the next 12 months**.

### **12\. I might assume that completing the survey places an order or that the product is guaranteed.**

**Problem found:** A crowdfunding product can make a hypothetical survey feel like a preorder or launch announcement.

**Change made:** The context and completion message both state that the exercise does not promise availability, guarantee production, or place an order.
