Instrument: Modular Card Wallet Demand Instrument
Reviewer: Conrad Rojas
Conrad reviewed the instrument and tested the form as a respondent. He confirmed that the six sections were in the correct order, the image loaded, and the valuation question included the unit, 12-month period, and option to enter zero.
His main concern was that the screeners could exclude possible customers. Conrad was screened out because he did not carry cards on three or more days each week, even though someone who carries cards less often could still buy a wallet. I broadened this question to ask whether respondents carry physical cards at least occasionally.
I also changed the question asking whether respondents might purchase or replace a wallet during the next 12 months. A “No” answer no longer ends the survey because that person may still have a low or zero willingness to pay. Both answers now continue to the valuation section.
Conrad suggested including the wallet’s dimensions and weight so respondents could better judge its size. I did not add the specifications to the description because I could not verify them.
Professor Hatch also pointed out that naming Ohsnap could lead respondents to search for the product and find its campaign price. I removed all brand references and now describe it as a modular card wallet. I also moved the awareness question to the end and changed it to:
Before today, had you seen a product like this one?
I did not change the valuation method because a wallet is normally purchased as one unit during the 12-month period. The question still asks for the maximum amount someone would pay for one bare-bones, cards-only modular wallet during the next 12 months, and the form continues to accept zero. I also kept the six sections in their original order because the reviewer confirmed that the structure was correct.
