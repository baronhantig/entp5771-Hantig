# Milestone 2 — Reconciliation of Inputs

## 1. Decision Period

My decision period remains 12 months, which is the same period I used in my Week 6 cost and scale analyses. My Milestone 1 survey also asked respondents how much they would pay for one bare-bones modular wallet during the next 12 months. Since both the demand and cost assumptions use the same period, no adjustment is necessary.

## 2. Unit

My unit remains one Bare Bones Ohsnap Wallet 2. In Week 6, I estimated the costs associated with producing and selling one wallet, and my demand survey asked respondents about their maximum willingness to pay for one wallet. These units are consistent, so no adjustment is needed.

## 3. Revised Reachable Population (N)

In Week 6, I used a reachable population of 124 people based on my University of Utah fraternity recruiting channel. At that point, I had six usable fraternity responses and two Reddit responses.

My completed Milestone 1 funnel showed 9 usable fraternity responses from 124 approaches and 30 additional responses attributed to Reddit from 311 post views. Overall, I collected 39 eligible responses from 42 submitted forms.

I am keeping **N = 124** as my primary reachable-population scenario because the fraternity remains the clearest group I can directly identify and approach. Although my Reddit post received 311 views, those views do not represent 311 unique, identifiable potential customers. Therefore, I cannot justify increasing N to 435 by combining fraternity approaches with Reddit views.

My Milestone 1 demand curve uses all 39 eligible respondents, including those attributed to Reddit. Applying that combined demand curve to the 124-person fraternity population assumes that the respondents' willingness to pay is reasonably representative of that population. I cannot verify this assumption because the survey did not record recruitment source for each response.

The final funnel changed my evidence about survey participation, but it did not establish a larger defensible reachable population. I therefore retain N = 124 while acknowledging that actual purchasing behavior could be substantially lower than stated willingness to pay.

## 4. Cost and Commitment Ranges

My Week 6 estimated variable cost was **$15.43–$24.59 per wallet**. This range includes manufacturing, packaging, shipping, payment processing, and expected returns or warranty replacements. These estimates are based on comparable products and public prices because Ohsnap's actual supplier costs are not available.

The original estimates included a $2.04 payment-processing fee based on a $60 sale. Because my Milestone 2 profit model will evaluate multiple prices, I will adjust that fee using the original benchmark rate of 2.9% plus $0.30 per transaction rather than assume the same fee at every price.

For fixed commitments, I will continue using the hypothetical **$500–$3,000 scenarios** from my Week 6 scale analysis. These are planning assumptions, not verified Ohsnap expenses. I do not have additional evidence that would justify replacing them with a more precise range.

My earlier cost analysis also identified a possible 300-unit manufacturing commitment of $1,815–$3,087 based on comparable suppliers. I will treat this as an upfront inventory and cash-risk issue rather than add it to fixed costs while also counting manufacturing in the variable cost of each wallet. Doing both would double-count manufacturing.

These assumptions allow me to test how profitability changes under different cost conditions without claiming to know Ohsnap's actual internal expenses.

## Conclusion

The period and unit remain consistent with Week 6. My final recruiting funnel provides updated participation evidence but does not justify increasing my reachable population beyond 124. I am also retaining the original variable-cost estimates and hypothetical fixed-commitment scenarios, with an adjustment for payment-processing costs at different prices.

The biggest uncertainties remain whether my survey respondents represent the reachable customer population, whether stated willingness to pay translates into purchases, and what Ohsnap's actual incremental fixed costs would be.
