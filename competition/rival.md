# Closest Rival

## 1. The Rival

The closest purchasable rival to the Bare Bones Ohsnap Wallet 2 is the
Secrid Cardprotector, made by Secrid. It currently sells for \$49 in the
United States. The Cardprotector is a slim aluminum wallet that holds up
to six cards and uses a built-in mechanism to slide the cards out for
quick access.

Product link: https://secrid.com/en-us/wallets/cardprotector/

## 2. Why This One

The Secrid Cardprotector serves the same basic customer as the Bare
Bones Wallet 2: someone who carries a small number of physical cards and
wants a slim wallet with quick card access. The products are also close
in price, with the Cardprotector at \$49 compared with the Wallet 2's
\$60 MSRP. A customer choosing between them would likely compare price,
card capacity, design, and the way each mechanism presents the cards.

## 3. Switching Costs

The biggest switching cost may be habit rather than money. Someone who
already owns a working wallet has to decide that faster card access or a
slimmer design is worth paying for a replacement before the old wallet
wears out. For someone already using a minimalist or pop-up wallet,
switching would also mean adjusting to a different card-access mechanism
and possibly a different card capacity.

## 4. Rival Price and Estimated Unit Cost

The Cardprotector's current U.S. retail price is \$49. Secrid does not
publish its manufacturing cost, so I estimate a per-unit cost of
approximately \$10--\$20. This is an inference rather than a reported
Secrid figure. A 2026 promotional-products distributor listed the
Cardprotector at a net distributor cost of £15.93 per unit for an order
of 100, which places an outside boundary above the manufacturer's
production cost. Secrid also reports that its wallets use anodized
aluminum, plastic, and stainless steel, with most components produced in
the Netherlands and others in Italy and final assembly performed in
Dutch social enterprises. I would refine this estimate if better
manufacturing-cost evidence becomes available before Week 12.

## 5. Stimulus Problem

I can describe the Cardprotector neutrally for the Week 10 instrument
as: A slim aluminum card wallet that holds up to six cards. A built-in
mechanism slides the cards out with one motion for quick access, and the
aluminum casing protects the cards while they are stored.

Secrid is an established retail wallet brand, so some respondents may
recognize the product even without its name or logo. I will flag that
possibility when designing the Week 10 instrument so that recognition
and an existing idea of its price can be accounted for.
